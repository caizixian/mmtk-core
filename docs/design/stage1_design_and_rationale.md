# Stage 1 Design and Rationale: Intra-Slice `unsafe` Refactoring in `mmtk-core`

Stage 1 refactored all six subsystem slices of the `mmtk-core` repository from the `ed89711964` baseline commit to the `ad1de8ffe3` merge commit. Six parallel slice workers audited 322 `.rs` files on Rust 1.84. Every slice kept external `pub` signatures and cross-folder `pub(crate)` signatures intact. Two adversarial review pairs checked every `unsafe` proof, rejected syntactic and atomic laundering, and guarded hot-path assembly before integration.

---

## 1. Summary and Quantitative Outcome

Stage 1 removed over 250 pre-existing incidental `unsafe` sites, removed all three `static mut` items, and documented every remaining `unsafe` boundary. Across all 322 `.rs` files, `unsafe impl` items fell by 72.6%, from 62 to 17. Total syntactic `unsafe` tokens in `.rs` code moved from 777 to 798 because Stage 1 promoted 75 net unchecked functions to `unsafe fn` signatures. Stage 1 also enabled the `-D unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn` lint flag crate-wide, which required narrow inner `unsafe {}` blocks inside `unsafe fn` bodies.

Three structural mechanisms explain why `unsafe impl` items dropped sharply although `unsafe fn` items grew:

1. **Auto-trait bounds removed 47 pre-existing `unsafe impl` items**. Earlier code added manual `Send` traits and `Sync` traits onto structs holding `UnsafeCell` fields, `RefCell` fields, raw pointers, or un-bounded trait objects. Stage 1 replaced those fields with safe atomics, `Mutex` locks, `RwLock` locks, `OnceLock` cells, `AtomicRefCell` cells, `usize` addresses, and `+ Sync` supertraits.
2. **Anti-laundering rules promoted 80+ unchecked helpers to `unsafe fn` signatures**. Slice workers promoted +54 helpers in the `Policy/Alloc/Copy` slice, +13 in the `Metadata` slice, +8 in the `VM/MockVM/DummyVM` slice, and +5 in the `Util` slice. Those helpers previously accepted raw `Address` values or `Block` handles behind safe `fn` signatures and dereferenced memory internally. After demoting five safe helpers in the `Heap` slice, the net +75 `unsafe fn` increase required explicit `unsafe {}` blocks at every call site.
3. **Crate-wide `unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn` rules narrowed every `unsafe fn` body**. On the `ed89711964` commit, `unsafe fn` bodies treated hundreds of lines of safe index math as implicitly `unsafe` code. Stage 1 wrapped only the exact dereference or call expression inside narrow `unsafe {}` blocks across all slices. That pass added +40 inner blocks in `Metadata`, +8 in `Util`, +7 in `MMTk/Plan/Scheduler`, and +2 in `VM` slices. It also added narrow inner blocks in the `Policy` slice and the `Heap` slice. All 547 library unit tests and Clippy safety lints now pass with zero warnings.

## 2. Taxonomy of Intrinsic vs Incidental `unsafe`

A garbage collector manages virtual memory, object headers, and stop-the-world phases that the `rustc` compiler cannot check statically. Stage 1 separated these intrinsic garbage-collection operations from incidental `unsafe` shortcuts inherited from JikesRVM's unboxed `org.vmmagic` design.

### 2.1 Five Intrinsic `unsafe` Categories Retained

Five core categories remain intrinsic to a garbage collector and account for the remaining `unsafe` items on the `ad1de8ffe3` commit:

1. **Operating-system virtual memory and C allocator FFI boundaries**. The `src/util/os` module maps heap chunks through the `mmap` syscall, the `munmap` syscall, the `mprotect` syscall, and the `madvise` syscall. Linux thread and mapping helpers also call the `prctl` syscall and the `sched_setaffinity` syscall. External allocator bindings call the `malloc` function, the `calloc` function, the `realloc` function, the `free` function, and the `malloc_usable_size` function. In addition, the `docs/dummyvm` crate exposes seven raw-pointer `pub unsafe extern "C" fn` entry points.
2. **Raw address dereferences, object headers, and VM slot accesses**. The `Address` type and the `ObjectReference` type project untyped memory words into typed loads, stores, and atomic references. The `MetadataValue` trait, the `SimpleSlot` struct, and the `<Range<Address> as MemorySlice>::copy` method implement these low-level memory accesses. The `HeaderMetadataSpec` struct and the `ObjectModel` trait also read and write sub-byte bitfields and forwarding words at VM-defined offsets.
3. **Memory-mapped side-metadata tables and intrusive free lists**. The `SideMetadataSpec` struct executes direct byte, word, and bulk memory operations over proportional virtual-address regions. In Immix, the `RCArray` wrapper views mapped reference-count pages as `&[[AtomicU8; BYTES_PER_LINE]; Block::LINES]` arrays. In the `native_ms` policy, dead cells store intrusive free-list pointers inside unallocated heap bytes alongside per-block side-metadata link tables.
4. **Zero-overhead unchecked table dispatch on inner GC loops**. During object tracing, the `SFTTrace::trace_object` method calls the `SFT_MAP.get_unchecked` method on every heap edge. That lookup relies on a two-word `transmute` call inside the `SFTRefStorage` struct to avoid bounds checks. Mutator fastpaths similarly index `Allocators` slots via `AllocatorSelector` tags without runtime tag-mismatch branches.
5. **Phase-synchronized `'static` lifetimes and cyclic worker ownership**. The `MMTK<VM>` root owns a leaked `NonNull<UnsafeCell<dyn Plan<VM = VM>>>` pointer whose `Prepare` phase, `Release` phase, and `finish_plan_pause` step rely on scheduler barriers. Policy spaces also promote `&self` borrows to `&'static` references for scoped GC work packets, and the `ScanMutatorRoots` packet borrows disjoint paused mutators. Finally, the `OpaquePointer` wrapper bridges opaque VM thread handles, and the `WorkerGroup` struct implements the `Sync` trait to break the `GCWorker` ownership cycle.

### 2.2 Five Incidental `unsafe` Categories Removed

Stage 1 removed 274 syntactic `unsafe` sites across five incidental categories:

1. **Manual `UnsafeCell` wrappers, `RefCell` wrappers, and `static mut` globals**. Slice workers replaced unsynchronized cells with `Mutex` locks, `RwLock` locks, `OnceLock` cells, `AtomicRefCell` cells, and `Arc` pointers. Hot flags and pointers moved to `AtomicPtr`, `AtomicBool`, `AtomicU8`, and `AtomicUsize` atomics across the `Map32`, `Map64`, `BlockPool`, `AllocationOptionsHolder`, `MatureEvecRemSet`, `AtomicPause`, and `MockVM` types.
2. **Redundant or unsound manual trait implementations**. Adding `+ Sync` bounds to the `BucketOpenCondition`, `WorkCounter`, and `FixtureContent` traits let the `rustc` compiler derive `Send` and `Sync` impls automatically. Storing a `plan_addr: usize` field in the `Prepare` and `Release` packets and a `PhantomData<fn() -> C>` marker in the `SchedulerStat` struct removed three more `unsafe impl` items. Removing the `bytemuck::PodInOption` impl on the `Pause` enum also closed an invalid discriminant cast in safe code.
3. **Uninitialized `MaybeUninit` arrays and slot wrappers**. Stage 1 replaced all 12 `MaybeUninit::uninit().assume_init()` array initializations in the `Allocators` struct and the `GCWorkerCopyContext` struct with the safe `std::array::from_fn` initializer. Replacing `MaybeUninit` fields with niche-optimized `Option` fields reduced the `GCTrigger` struct to zero `unsafe` items. The same change cut the `src/util/copy/mod.rs` file from 18 `unsafe` blocks to one compatibility method.
4. **Provenance-stripping type casts and lifetime `transmute` macros**. On the baseline commit, the `define_erased_vm_mut_ref!` macro cast `&mut GCWorker<VM>` borrows through `usize` integers. The `MockVM` harness also expanded a `lifetime!` `std::mem::transmute` macro across eight callback sites. Stage 1 replaced both patterns with safe `&'a mut (dyn Any + 'static)` downcasts and higher-ranked `MockMethodFn` trait objects.
5. **Avoidable `Address` fabrications, unchecked cold SFT queries, and manual test allocations**. Slice workers replaced `Address::zero()` calls and `Address::from_usize(0)` calls with the safe `Address::ZERO` constant, and switched cold `ObjectReference` state queries to the `SFT_MAP.get_checked` method. Inline fixed-size arrays in the `SFTSpaceMap` struct and the `SFTDenseChunkMap` struct removed six `get_unchecked` calls. In tests and benchmarks, stack-aligned structs and `&'a Atomic<T>` borrows replaced leaked `alloc_zeroed` buffers and raw-pointer slot wrappers.

## 3. Subsystem Design Decisions and Trade-offs

Each slice rejected naive safe wrappers when those wrappers added hot-path branches, spilled registers, or masked real aliasing and memory-ordering bugs on the `master` branch.

### 3.1 `Heap` and `PageResource` (`67 -> 34` Code `unsafe`)

#### Static-Default `AtomicPtr` Leaf Load in `vm_layout` vs `OnceLock::get_or_init`

On the `master` branch, two `static mut VM_LAYOUT` globals (for 32-bit and 64-bit targets) exposed release builds to `&'static` aliasing and data-race undefined behavior (UB). A naive `OnceLock::get_or_init` replacement added an `Acquire` state load and a cold branch inside the `SFTSpaceMap::get_unchecked` method on every traced object. That branch also spilled three callee-saved registers (`%r15`, `%r14`, and `%rbx`) onto the stack (review #22).

Instead, the `vm_layout.rs` file pairs an immutable `DEFAULT_VM_LAYOUT` static, a `CUSTOM_VM_LAYOUT: OnceLock<VMLayout>` cell, and a `VM_LAYOUT_PTR: AtomicPtr<VMLayout>` pointer initialized to the `DEFAULT_VM_LAYOUT` static. Custom setup publishes the pointer with `Ordering::Release` ordering, and the `vm_layout()` function reads it with `Ordering::Acquire` ordering as a branchless leaf load. Caching the `heap_end` field and the `log_space_extent` field on the `Map64` struct also made the `Map64::get_descriptor_for_address` method a seven-instruction stackless leaf with zero register spills.

#### Fine-Grained Atomics and Per-Space Locks in `Map32` and `Map64` vs `UnsafeCell`

On the `master` branch, the `Map32::allocate_contiguous_chunks` method held a `self_mut: &mut Map32Inner` borrow across a `self.insert(...)` call. That call created simultaneous `&mut` and `&` borrows of `Map32Inner` (Stacked Borrows UB), and concurrent readers read `UnsafeCell` fields on `Map32Inner` and `Map64Inner` without synchronization.

Stage 1 removed every `UnsafeCell` wrapper in the `src/util/heap/` directory. The `Map32` struct split into a `Mutex<Map32Sync>` lock, a `OnceLock<IntArrayFreeList>` cell, and a `Box<[AtomicSpaceDescriptor]>` slice. The `Map64` struct split into `[AtomicSpaceDescriptor; MAX_SPACES]` and `[Mutex<SpaceState>; MAX_SPACES]` arrays. Narrowing the `VMMap::create_freelist` receiver to `&'static self` prevented non-`'static` parent dangling pointers at compile time. Adding the `SpaceState::freelist_created` guard also stopped duplicate `RawMemoryFreeList` instances from creating aliasing `&'static mut [i32]` slices (reviews #22 and #23).

#### Weak-Memory Ordering, Release Bounds Guards, and Lock-Free `BlockPool`

Stage 1 also fixed three latent heap soundness hazards without slowing page or block allocation:

- **ARM64 slab publication ordering**. In the `TwoLevelStateStorage` struct, the `OnceOptionBox::get_or_init` method allocates a `Slab` buffer via the `alloc()` function and writes `MapState::Unmapped` bytes before publishing the pointer. Using `Ordering::Relaxed` loads let ARM64 readers observe uninitialized heap bytes (review #23). Stage 1 upgraded the `slab_table` load to `Ordering::Acquire` ordering and the `get_or_allocate_slab_table` compare-exchange to `(Ordering::Acquire, Ordering::Release)` ordering.
- **Release shift and contiguous guards**. In the `SpaceDescriptor::get_start` method and the `get_start_32` helper, unconditional release `is_contiguous()` assertions and `< usize::BITS as usize` shift checks replaced debug-only checks. Those checks also handled 64-bit compressed heaps larger than 4 GiB.
- **Lock-free `BlockPool::pop` and atomic `ChunkMap` metadata**. The `ChunkMap` table switched to `Ordering::Relaxed` atomic loads and stores to fix concurrent `set_allocated` races. In addition, the `BlockQueue` struct replaced its `UnsafeCell<Box<[MaybeUninit<B>]>>` buffer with an immutable `Box<[B]>` slice, an `AtomicUsize` countdown cursor, and `CachePadded<spin::Mutex<Vec<B>>>` queues at zero `unsafe` cost.

### 3.2 `Util` (`97 -> 92` Code `unsafe`, 25 Pre-Existing Sites Removed)

#### Two-Register `&'a mut (dyn Any + 'static)` in `erase_vm.rs` vs `usize` or `(NonNull<()>, TypeId)`

On the `master` branch, the `define_erased_vm_mut_ref!` macro cast a `&mut GCWorker<VM1>` borrow to a `usize` integer. It then rebuilt a `&mut GCWorker<VM2>` borrow via the `with_exposed_provenance` function without checking the `VM2` type, allowing silent cross-VM transmutation in safe code. A manual `(NonNull<()>, TypeId)` pair takes 24 bytes on 64-bit targets, which exceeds the 16-byte two-register SysV x86-64 ABI limit. That 24-byte struct spilled onto the stack on every `SFT::trace_object` call (review #29). Storing a `&'a mut (dyn Any + 'static)` fat reference and calling the `downcast_mut` method keeps the wrapper at 16 bytes in two registers. This representation causes zero stack spills, preserves mutable provenance, enforces lifetime invariance, and needs zero `unsafe` blocks.

#### Checked SFT Queries on `ObjectReference` vs `SFT_MAP.get_unchecked`

The `ObjectReference::from_raw_address` constructor is a safe `pub fn` item, and the `ObjectReference` type allows out-of-space VM addresses for the `ActivePlan::vm_trace_object` hook. However, six safe `ObjectReference` methods (`is_reachable`, `is_live`, `is_movable`, `get_forwarded_object`, `is_in_any_space`, and `is_sane`) previously called the `SFT_MAP.get_unchecked` method. Passing an out-of-space `ObjectReference` value in `--release` builds caused out-of-bounds slice indexing UB on the `SFTSparseChunkMap` table. It also caused unmapped side-metadata reads on the `SFTDenseChunkMap` table (review #26). None of those six methods runs on the inner tracing loop (which calls `SFT_MAP.get_unchecked` directly inside `SFTTrace::trace_object`), and each already performs dynamic vtable calls. Switching all six methods to the safe `SFT_MAP.get_checked` lookup removed six `unsafe` blocks and closed all six UB holes.

#### Concurrency, Freelist, and Allocator UB Fixes in `src/util`

Auditing the `src/util` modules uncovered and fixed six additional UB bugs on the `master` branch:

- **Parent use-after-free (UAF) and `&mut Vec<i32>` aliasing in `IntArrayFreeList`**. The `IntArrayFreeList` struct previously stored a raw `Option<NonNull<IntArrayFreeList>>` parent pointer with manual `Send` and `Sync` impls. That pointer caused UAF if the parent dropped first and `&mut Vec<i32>` races across discontiguous `FreeListPageResource` children. Replacing the table with a shared `Arc<RwLock<Vec<i32>>>` lock removed all four `unsafe` items in the `int_array_freelist.rs` file.
- **Unconstrained `Send` and `Sync` auto-traits in `OnceOptionBox` and `InitializeOnce`**. Adding a `PhantomData<OnceLock<T>>` marker and `at_least_acquire` and `at_least_release` orderings fixed `!Send` smuggling and ARM64 data races in `OnceOptionBox<T>`. A private `unsafe trait InitializeOnceSync` bound sealed `InitializeOnce<T>: Sync` and evaluated the `init_fn()` closure before creating the `&mut *self.v.get()` borrow (review #26).
- **Zero-size allocation and null `write_bytes` UB**. Early returns on the `size == 0 || size_of::<T>() == 0` condition in the `new_zeroed_vec` function stopped zero-byte `alloc_zeroed` calls. Early returns on the `len == 0` condition in the `memory::set` function stopped null `ptr::write_bytes` calls. In addition, `Address` dereference debug assertions now reject zero addresses even for zero-sized types.
- **Buffer underflow and provenance loss in `malloc_ms_util`**. On 64-bit targets (`BYTES_IN_ADDRESS == 8`), calling the `malloc_ms_util::align_offset_alloc` function with `align == 4` previously added `align` one time. That calculation wrote the offset pointer 4 bytes before the `calloc` allocation. Looping until the `result >= address + BYTES_IN_ADDRESS` condition holds with `checked_add` guards and `*mut c_void` `write_unaligned` and `read_unaligned` calls fixed both bugs.
- **Subtraction underflow in `RawMemoryFreeList` and `CPU_SETSIZE` bounds**. Fixing the `self.high_water - self.limit` expression to `self.limit - self.high_water` and promoting release bounds checks hardened the `RawMemoryFreeList` constructor. Asserting the `cpu_idx < libc::CPU_SETSIZE as usize` bound in the `linux_common.rs` file also closed syscall bitmask overruns.
- **Concurrent straddle-line races in `rc.rs`**. The `mark_straddle_object_with_size` method, the `unmark_straddle_object` method, and straddle-line queries previously updated the `RC_TABLE` table with non-atomic `RC_TABLE.store` byte writes. Switching those paths to `load_atomic` and `store_atomic` (`Ordering::Relaxed`) calls removed lost reference-count updates across parallel LXR workers.

### 3.3 `Metadata` (`297 -> 292` Code `unsafe`, 58 Pre-Existing Blocks Removed)

#### Provenance-Preserving `SIDE_METADATA_BASE_PTR` vs `unwrap_unchecked` or `AtomicUsize`

On the `master` branch, the `global_side_metadata_base_address()` function called the `SIDE_METADATA_BASE_ADDRESS.get().unwrap_unchecked()` method, which caused instant UB if called before initialization. Replacing that cell with an `AtomicUsize` counter plus an `Address::ZERO + raw` offset strips pointer provenance and launders integer-to-`Address` fabrication (review #33). Instead, the `side_metadata/layout.rs` file stores a `SIDE_METADATA_BASE_PTR: AtomicPtr<u8>` pointer with `Ordering::Release` ordering during `initialize_side_metadata_base` and reads it via `Address::from_mut_ptr(SIDE_METADATA_BASE_PTR.load(Ordering::Acquire))`. This design reduced the `side_metadata/layout.rs` file to zero `unsafe` items and kept full pointer provenance. It also synchronized ARM64 readers (review #28) and compiled to a single `mov` instruction on x86-64.

#### Unconditional `assert_spec` Checks and 8-Byte `SIDE_METADATA_SPEC_ALIGN` Alignment

Both the `HeaderMetadataSpec` struct and the `SideMetadataSpec` struct keep `pub` fields so external VM bindings can construct `const` struct literals. On 32-bit x86 and ARM targets, the `BYTES_IN_WORD` constant is 4 whereas the `align_of::<AtomicU64>()` alignment is 8. Using 4-byte alignment in the `side_metadata_offset_after` function and unaligned `global_side_metadata_bytes` totals created misaligned `&AtomicU64` borrows (instant UB). Gating the `assert_spec` check and the `assert_value_type` check on the `#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` attribute also stripped type-width checks in `--release` builds (review #28).

Stage 1 aligned spec offsets and bases to the 8-byte `SIDE_METADATA_SPEC_ALIGN` constant (`max(BYTES_IN_WORD, align_of::<AtomicU64>())`). It also promoted spec-shape, `max_metadata_align::<T>()`, and `bcopy_metadata_contiguous` contiguous-layout checks to unconditional release `assert!` macros. For `const` metadata specs, LLVM folds these checks to zero instructions and guarantees release-build soundness.

#### RMW Ordering Adapter, `MetadataByteArrayRef` De-Aliasing, and Concurrency Fixes

Stage 1 fixed five additional soundness and memory-leak bugs across the `metadata` and `bulk_meta` directories:

- **Sub-byte and masked RMW ordering panics**. Emulated RMW loops in the `HeaderMetadataSpec` and `SideMetadataSpec` types (`store_atomic`, `compare_exchange*`, `fetch_add*`, and `fetch_sub*` methods) passed `Ordering::Release` and `Ordering::AcqRel` orderings to the `MetadataValue::load_atomic` method. They also passed those orderings as the `fetch_order` argument of the `fetch_update` method, violating `# Safety` contracts and panicking at runtime (review #28). Adding the `const fn load_order_for_rmw(order: Ordering) -> Ordering` helper fixed all 10 emulated RMW paths alongside an unshifted sub-byte failure return in the `HeaderMetadataSpec::compare_exchange` method.
- **`'static` slice aliasing in `MetadataByteArrayRef`**. Replacing the `data: &'static [u8; ENTRIES]` field (which aliased concurrent `Line::mark` writes) with a `meta_start: Address` field and bounds-checked byte loads removed reference-aliasing UB.
- **Data races in `PinningBit`, `vo_bit`, and `bcopy_metadata_contiguous`**. Switching the `PinningBit::is_object_pinned` method to `load_atomic` (`Ordering::SeqCst`) and switching the `get_object_ref_for_vo_addr` function to `from_raw_address` plus an atomic debug check removed two read races. Updating partial boundary bytes via atomic `fetch_update` loops in the `bcopy_metadata_contiguous` function removed a third concurrent race.
- **Subobject provenance and page-zero benchmark hazards**. Calling the `to_mut_ptr::<c_void>()` method instead of taking a 1-byte `as_mut_ref()` borrow before `libc::memset` calls in the `bulk_meta/bzero_bset.rs` file preserved full-array provenance. Offsetting the `bulk_meta/access.rs` benchmark from the `BENCH_DATA_BASE` (`0x1000_0000`) constant also avoided page-zero `dzmmap` mapping conflicts.
- **Leaked `alloc_zeroed` test buffers**. Stack-allocated `TestHeaderObject<T>` values, stack `u32` and `u64` words, and `#[repr(C, align(...))]` `Box` structs (`AlignedBitmap`, `AlignedLineMeta`, and `AlignedBlockMeta` types) replaced 25 `alloc_zeroed` blocks. Those safe allocations also fixed test memory leaks.

### 3.4 `Policy`, `Alloc`, and `Copy` (`143 -> 282` Code `unsafe`, 61 Pre-Existing Sites Removed)

#### Niche-Optimized `Option` Slots in `GCWorkerCopyContext` vs `[MaybeUninit; 1]` Arrays

On the `master` branch, the `GCWorkerCopyContext` struct and the `Allocators` struct built `[MaybeUninit; 1]` and multi-slot arrays via `MaybeUninit::uninit().assume_init()` (12 immediate-UB sites). In addition, the `GCWorkerCopyContext` struct exposed `pub` `MaybeUninit` fields that safe code could overwrite with `MaybeUninit::uninit()` values. Stage 1 replaced those fields with private `Option<ImmixCopyContext<VM>>` and `Option<ImmixHybridCopyContext<VM>>` fields plus a `CopySpaceCopySlot(Option<CopySpaceCopyContext<VM>>)` wrapper, removing all 18 `unsafe` blocks in the `src/util/copy/mod.rs` file. The `GCWorkerCopyContext::new` constructor also asserts the `MAX_COPYSPACE_COPY_ALLOCATORS == 1` invariant and the `idx == 0` bound. Those assertions let the `alloc_copy` and `post_copy` methods index the `self.copy.0[0]` slot without a redundant `index < 1` check on every copy (reviews #39 and #61).

#### In-Place `BlockList` Unlinking vs `BLOCK_LIST_TABLE` Back-Pointers

In the `native_ms` policy, every `Block` handle previously stored a raw `*mut BlockList` pointer inside the `BLOCK_LIST_TABLE` side table. Calling the `attempt_release` method synthesized an aliasing `&mut *block_list` borrow when `FreeListAllocator` or `AbandonedBlockLists` already held a `&mut BlockList` borrow (Stacked and Tree Borrows UB). Moving a `Mutator<VM>` struct also left those back-pointers dangling, and the `BlockList::append` method had to rewrite all O(N) back-pointers.

Stage 1 removed the `store_block_list`, `load_block_list`, and `attempt_release` methods and made the `BlockList::append` method O(1). It also made the `BlockList` `size` field and `FreeListAllocator` fields private with `#[repr(C)]` layout notes (policy #52). In the `native_ms/block.rs` file, Stage 1 promoted 64 KiB alignment checks to release `assert!` guards and clamped `naive_brute_force_sweep` candidates to `[cell, cell + cell_size)`. Sweep helpers now query side mark bits by `Address` instead of fabricating `ObjectReference` values on unallocated free-list link words (review #62).

#### `RCArray`, Inline `SFTMap` Tables, `MallocSpace`, `Lisp2Space`, and Allocator Fixes

Across the remaining space policies and allocators, Stage 1 resolved seven concrete UB and concurrency defects:

- **Immix `RCArray` `'static [u8]` aliasing and line-mark races**. The `RCArray` wrapper previously materialized a `&'static [[u8; BYTES_PER_LINE]; Block::LINES]` borrow over `RC_TABLE` bytes mutated by concurrent `AtomicU8` updates. Redesigning the `RCArray<'a>` type over `&'a [[AtomicU8; BYTES_PER_LINE]; Block::LINES]` slices with atomic `RCArray::clear` stores and release `assert_rc_table_mapped` checks fixed the race and cut ~100 lines of `UInt*` boilerplate. ### 3.5 `MMTk`, `Plan`, and `Scheduler` (`91 -> 36` Code `unsafe`, `-60.4%`)

#### Leaked `NonNull<UnsafeCell<dyn Plan>>` in `MMTK::plan` vs `Box` Wrappers

On the `master` branch, the `MMTK::plan` field had type `UnsafeCell<Box<dyn Plan<VM = VM>>>`, which wrapped the `Box` pointer rather than the heap `Plan` pointee. Moving the `plan` field or reborrowing `Box`'s `Unique` pointer invalidated `'static` borrows in the `SFT_MAP`, `GCTrigger`, `Mutator`, and `Allocators` types (review #45).

Switching to an owned `Box<UnsafeCell<dyn Plan<VM = VM>>>` wrapper fixed the `Unique` retag hazard. Yet dropping an `MMTK<VM>` instance freed the `Plan` pointee when the global `SFT_MAP` table still held `'static` space pointers for `memory_manager::is_mmtk_object` calls (review #68). Converting the `Box<UnsafeCell<dyn Plan<VM = VM>>>` pointer after boot setup via `NonNull::new(Box::into_raw(plan)).unwrap()` calls gives the `Plan` pointee `SharedReadWrite` provenance and a genuine `'static` lifetime in two words.

#### Pause-End Borrow Splitting and Safe Public Entry-Point Guards

In the `GCWorkScheduler::on_gc_finished` method, every plan's `on_pause_end(&mut self, ...)` implementation previously called `mmtk.gc_trigger.policy.on_gc_end(mmtk)` (and generational plans called `gc_trigger.get_min_nursery_pages()`) while `plan_mut: &mut dyn Plan` stayed live on the stack. Those nested calls invoked the `mmtk.get_plan()` and `gc_trigger.plan()` methods, creating aliasing `&mut dyn Plan` and `&dyn Plan` borrows (review #45). Splitting plan mutation into the `finish_plan_pause` method and pre-computing the `compute_next_gc_full_heap` flag let `plan_mut` drop before `on_gc_end` runs. Stage 1 also added a one-shot `SFT_INITIALIZED: AtomicBool` guard inside the `Plan::initialize_sft(&self)` method so safe callers cannot alias `&mut dyn SFTMap` borrows. In addition, an `assert!(std::ptr::eq(self.mmtk, mmtk))` check at the entry of the `GCWorker::run` method stops cross-instance workers from triggering uninitialized `copy[0]` UB (review #44).

#### LXR Aliasing, Memory-Ordering, and Poisoning Fixes Across 20 Newly Safe Files

Stage 1 reduced 20 `.rs` files in the `src/plan/` and `src/scheduler/` directories to zero `unsafe` items. Those files include all nine plan `mutator.rs` files, the `lxr/barrier.rs`, `lxr/block_allocation.rs`, `lxr/mature_evac.rs`, and `lxr/gc_work/{mod,prepare,tracing}.rs` files, and the `scheduler/stat.rs` file:

Replacing three `*mut GCWorker<VM>` fields in LXR tracing with `OptionObjectQueue` queues and `&mut GCWorker<VM>` arguments removed worker-borrow aliasing. Decoupling `BlockAllocation` self-references via `Box::leak` plus an `AtomicBool` flag removed a third Stacked Borrows hazard. Deferring `LAZY_SWEEPING_JOBS` registration to the `initialize_collection(&'static self)` method fixed the fourth hazard (review #45).
- **Zero-allocation safe compaction and thread-local remsets**. Replacing `ProcessIncs` `ManuallyDrop` pointer writes with `incs.into_iter().filter_map(...).collect::<Vec<_>>()` chains reused matching `Vec` buffers in place via stdlib `in_place_collect` specialization without unwind leaks. Replacing `MatureEvecRemSet` `UnsafeCell` buffers with `CachePadded<AtomicRefCell<...>>` cells and `Mutex` locks removed nine `unsafe` items (review #44).

Uniform 1-byte `AtomicU8` (`Ordering::Relaxed`) stores fixed mixed-size and non-atomic `unlog_field_relaxed` races on recycled Immix lines. Safe slot iteration also removed all `barrier.rs` `unsafe` blocks (review #45).

Guarding the `o.to_raw_address() + size_of::<usize>() <= obj_end` bound in the `rc.rs` and `mature_sweeping.rs` files prevented overwriting adjacent live headers on 4-byte-aligned or offset objects (review #45).

### 3.6 `VM`, `MockVM`, and `DummyVM` (`82 -> 62` `.rs` Code `unsafe`, 35 Sites Removed)

#### Module-Private `HashMap` Safepoint Registry Plus Lock-Free Fixture Fastpath in `MockVM`

On the `master` branch, the `VMMutatorThread::as_mock_mutator` method was a safe `pub fn` item that cast `self.0` (`VMThread`) directly to a `*mut MutatorHandle` pointer. That cast allowed null or forged-pointer dereference UB in safe Rust. Meanwhile, the `GenericMutatorFixture::mutator(&self)` method handed out aliasing `&'static mut Mutator<VM>` borrows from `&self` borrows, and public `MUTATOR_PARK` methods let callers forge registrations (review #24). Checking a `Mutex<HashMap<VMThread, ...>>` table on every `fixture.mutator()` call added two locks and two probes per 8-byte bump allocation in the `benches/mock_bench/alloc.rs` benchmark (review #37). Stage 1 solved both requirements with a two-tier design:

- **Cold GC safepoint path**. The module-private `MUTATOR_PARK` registry maps `VMThread` keys to `MutatorHandleRef(&'static MutatorHandle)` values in safe Rust alongside a private `GC_THREADS` set. That registry removes raw `VMThread` pointer casts and blocks registration forgery.
- **Hot allocation benchmark path**. The `GenericMutatorFixture<VM>` struct stores its `MutatorHandleRef` handle directly and returns `&mut Mutator<VM>` borrows from `GenericMutatorFixture::mutator(&mut self)` calls. Fastpath calls check `active_thread: AtomicUsize` and `is_parked: AtomicBool` (`Ordering::Acquire` ordering) with zero `Mutex` locks and zero `HashMap` probes. Calls lock `transfer_owner` only if a fixture moves across OS threads, so another thread cannot spoof-park an active mutator (reviews #24 and #37).

- **Atomic `<Address as Slot>` and byte-granular `<Range<Address> as MemorySlice>::copy`**. In the `src/vm/slot.rs` file, delegating the `<Address as Slot>` impl to `SimpleSlot` (`Atomic<Address>` with `Ordering::Relaxed` ordering) fixed duplicate-edge tracing data races. Switching the `<Range<Address> as MemorySlice>::copy` method from word-aligned `ptr::copy::<usize>` calls to byte-granular `std::ptr::copy::<u8>` calls with release `end >= start`, length-equality, `bytes == 0`, and `!is_null()` guards fixed 1-byte array misalignment. ## 4. Anti-Laundering Rules and Adversarial Review Catches

Stage 1 paired every slice author with two reviewers: a safety reviewer (`@safety-a` or `@safety-b` worker) and an anti-laundering reviewer (`@launder-a` or `@launder-b` worker). Safety reviewers checked every UB precondition and `// SAFETY:` claim line by line. Anti-laundering reviewers rejected syntactic tricks, un-checked safe wrappers, and hot-path regressions across reviews #22 through #74.

### 4.1 Three Concrete Laundering Patterns Rejected

1. **Bypassing the `Address::from_usize` constructor via `Address::ZERO + raw` math (reviews #33 and #39)**. Following VEE 2009 (`org.vmmagic` design), the `Address::from_usize(raw: usize)` constructor is intentionally `pub const unsafe fn` so fabricating an `Address` value from an integer leaves an auditable `unsafe` site. In Round 1 of the `swarm/meta` branch, 38 non-zero sites replaced `unsafe { Address::from_usize(raw) }` calls with `Address::ZERO + raw` or `Address::ZERO.add(raw)` expressions. Those sites included the `ranges::tests::mk_addr` helper, the `contiguous_meta_address_to_address` function, and hardcoded non-zero test constants. The `@launder-a` reviewer rejected all 38 sites and also rejected a Stage-2 proposal for a blanket safe `Address::from_raw_bits(usize)` wrapper (review #39). Genuine zero sentinels kept the `Address::ZERO` constant, and the `SIDE_METADATA_BASE_PTR` global switched to `AtomicPtr<u8>` plus `Address::from_mut_ptr` (`0` `unsafe` items with real pointer provenance). Stride offsets from a base `Address` constant kept `+ i * DATA_STRIDE` offsets, whereas raw integer fabrications restored explicit `unsafe { Address::from_usize(...) }` blocks.
2. **Swapping non-atomic `load` and `store` calls to `*_atomic` calls in sequential tests (review #33)**. The `HeaderMetadataSpec` and `SideMetadataSpec` structs still expose safe `pub fn *_atomic` methods only because Stage 1 kept external `pub` signatures intact. Internally, those methods still dereference un-checked raw `Address` arguments. In Round 1 of the `swarm/meta` branch, 37 sites in single-threaded unit tests (`header_metadata.rs`, `side_metadata/global.rs`, and `side_metadata_tests.rs` files) swapped `unsafe { spec.load / spec.store }` calls to `spec.load_atomic / spec.store_atomic(..., Ordering::SeqCst)` calls. The `@launder-a` reviewer rejected those 37 swaps because they hid un-checked dereferences and removed white-box checks between `store_atomic` writes and non-atomic `load` reads. Concurrent GC paths in the `pin_bit.rs` file and the `vo_bit/mod.rs` file kept atomic loads, whereas sequential unit tests restored documented `unsafe { spec.load / spec.store }` blocks.
3. **Leaving un-checked preconditions or `debug_assert!`-only guards inside safe `fn` items (reviews #22 to #28, #44, #45, #61, #62, and #68)**. Under RustBelt semantic soundness, a safe function cannot rely on prose comments or `debug_assert!` checks (which vanish in `--release` builds) to prevent UB. Reviewers caught and fixed both forms of this defect:
   - **Internal helpers with caller-only preconditions**. Reviewers required `unsafe fn` signatures on the `VMMap::free_all_chunks`, `CommonPageResource::release_all_chunks`, and `MonotonePageResource::release_pages` chain (review #22). They enforced the same rule on 54 policy and allocator helpers, 13 metadata helpers, and 5 util helpers.
   - **Safe public or cross-module APIs callable with adversarial inputs**. Where signatures had to stay safe, reviewers required release-mode runtime checks. Added guards include the one-shot `SFT_INITIALIZED: AtomicBool` flag in `Plan::initialize_sft` (reviews #44 and #45) and the `ptr::eq(self.mmtk, mmtk)` check in `GCWorker::run` (review #44). Other release guards include `SFT_MAP.get_checked` lookups on `ObjectReference` (review #26) and the `Lisp2PassGuard` lock plus header-prefix bounds checks in `Lisp2Space` (reviews #61 and #62). Reviewers also added list-membership checks in the `CommonPageResource::release_discontiguous_chunks` method (review #23) and `checked_add` guards in `VMSpace` and bump allocators (reviews #61 and #62). Finally, sealed `MUTATOR_PARK` and `active_thread` checks guarded the `MockVM` harness (review #24).

### 4.2 How Stage-1 Honest Boundaries Set Up Stage 2

Stage 1 prohibited cross-folder signature edits so six slice branches could merge in parallel without conflicts. That merge needed only the three-line `let mut fixture` binding update in the `ad1de8ffe3` commit. Instead of hiding cross-folder invariants behind un-checked safe wrappers, Stage 1 turned implicit prose assumptions into 173 compiler-checked `unsafe fn` boundaries with exact `/// # Safety` contracts.

Those explicit contracts map directly onto the cross-folder Stage-2 type-system capabilities vetted in the `cross_boundary_design.md` blueprint:

- **Linear block typestates (`SweptBlock` and `UnsweptBlock` types) and sealed `BlockLists` wrappers**. The `native_ms` module and the `FreeListAllocator` struct hold 124 `unsafe` items, including 54 un-laundered `unsafe fn` items. Every `# Safety` contract there restates block alignment, mapped metadata, and list ownership. Encoding linear block ownership across sealed bins takes the `FreeListAllocator` struct to zero `unsafe` items. That change also removes the unused `MS_BLOCK_LIST` side table and saves 8 bytes of side metadata per 64 KiB block.
- **Space-sealed region witnesses (`MappedBlock` and `MallocExclusiveChunk` types) and shared `AtomicMetadataOps` dispatch**. The `unsafe fn` boundaries in the `metadata`, `immix`, and `malloc_ms` modules collapse into space-branded safe projections (`MappedBlock::{rc_table, line_mark_table}` and `MallocExclusiveChunk::tables_mut` methods). Those projections use 8-byte-aligned `bytemuck::cast_slice::<u8, [u64; 2]>` slices alongside deduplicated `resolve_atomic` helpers.
- **Bounded ordinals (`Fin<N>` type) and lock-free `AtomicPtr<SFTMapImpl>` dispatch**. Stage 1's inline `[SFTRefStorage; 32]` and `[SFTRefStorage; 256]` arrays plus the `InitializeOnceSync` seal let Stage 2 remove the `InitializeOnce<T>` type and keep `SFT_MAP.get_unchecked` branch-free.
- **Instance-branded phase and mutator capabilities (`MmtkId`, `StwCell`, `WorkersParkedToken`, `MutatorTicket`, and `InitSelectorSet` types)**. Stage 1's leaked `NonNull<UnsafeCell<dyn Plan>>` pointer, `MutatorBuilder` checks, and `GCWorker::run` identity check let Stage 2 remove internal `MMTK::get_plan_mut` calls. Those tokens also de-alias `ScanMutatorRoots` borrows from `PrepareMutator` borrows and seal `#[repr(C)] Allocators<VM>` slots behind a `u16` `InitSelectorSet` bitmask (policy #52). Finally, Stage 2 removes redundant `Space` `Sync` impls because the `BlockPageResource` struct is now safe.
